About this game

Shadows over Geneva is a decision game for Cambridge IGCSE History (Core Content Option B: international relations 1919–39) and Cambridge International AS & A Level History (the League of Nations and international relations in the 1920s and 1930s).

Players are scored on whether they choose what actually happened, not what they think should have happened. After every decision the game explains why the other options were not chosen, which makes it a good starting point for discussion.

In the classroom

  • Pairs on one tablet: one student argues for an option, the other against, before sealing the decision.
  • Use the end-of-game debrief as an exam-practice list: pick one failure and one success of the League and explain why.
  • A-Level: use the Historian’s Desk notes as essay starters on debates over Hitler’s intentions, appeasement and the League.
  • Progress is saved on the device, so a game can continue next lesson.
  • Tap ⛶ Full screen (or press F) to hide the browser bars. On iPhone, use Share → Add to Home Screen and open the game from its icon.

A note on names

The League’s inner body was called the Council. The term “Security Council” belongs to the United Nations (1945). In this game the Council is described as the League’s “security council” to make the link.
